# The page brief

Fill this in before drafting. A brief takes ten minutes and saves the rewrite that happens when a
page turns out to target the wrong intent or another page's keyword.

## Contents

- Template
- Finding the intent
- Choosing terms
- Collecting buyer questions
- Collecting facts
- Worked example (fictional)

## Template

```text
Page:            URL and page type (homepage, service, guide, comparison, tool)
Intent:          what the searcher wants to do or know, in one sentence
Primary term:    the one search term this page owns (with volume, source, date)
Secondary terms: close variants this page also carries
Not this page:   terms other pages own, and which page owns each
Buyer questions: 4 to 6 real questions, with where each came from
Facts available: each fact with its source and date
Proof available: logos, ratings, awards, case studies the business has cleared for public use
Author/reviewer: real names: as on the current page, or ask who for a new page
Supports:        the commercial page this guide links to (guides only)
H1:              draft
Title:           draft, about 30 to 60 characters
Description:     draft, about 70 to 160 characters
H2s:             draft list
```

## Finding the intent

Search the primary term and look at the top 10. What kind of page wins (service pages, guides,
lists, tools) tells you what the searcher wants. Write the intent as a task: "compare wholesale
bread suppliers in Austin before asking for quotes", not "bakery content".

## Choosing terms

- The primary term comes from the site's term ownership table. If there is no table, ask for one or
  build it with the `seo-geo-website-design` skill first.
- Secondary terms are variants that share most of their top 10 results with the primary term.
- List the terms this page must not target and the page that owns each. You will link to those
  pages with those terms as anchors.

## Collecting buyer questions

Good sources, in order of trust:

1. Questions customers asked in sales calls, emails and support tickets.
2. Google's "People also ask" for the primary term and its variants.
3. Related searches and autocomplete.
4. Community threads where buyers ask each other.

Keep the buyer's wording. Drop any question another page owns, and link to it instead.

## Collecting facts

- Every fact gets a source and a date in the brief. If a fact has neither, it does not go on the
  page.
- Own facts (the business's data, method, prices it publishes, people) are worth more than
  third-party statistics, because nobody else can say them.
- Client names, logos, numbers and quotes need the client's permission before they go public.
  If you do not know whether permission exists, leave them out and ask.

## Worked example (fictional)

Larkspur Oven is a fictional bakery used only to show the format.

```text
Page:            /wholesale (service page)
Intent:          a restaurant owner wants a reliable early-morning bread supplier in Austin
Primary term:    wholesale bakery austin ([volume], [tool], [month year])
Secondary terms: bread supplier for restaurants
Not this page:   bakery in austin (owned by /), custom cakes austin (owned by /custom-cakes)
Buyer questions: What is the minimum order? (sales calls) | Do you deliver on Sundays? (support)
                 | Can I get a sample box? (sales calls) | How far ahead must I order? (PAA)
Facts available: delivery before 7 am for orders by 9 pm (the business's delivery terms, 2026)
Proof available: none cleared yet; leave the logo strip out
Author/reviewer: the head baker (author), the owner (reviewer)
H1:              Wholesale bread for Austin restaurants, delivered before 7 am
Title:           Wholesale bakery in Austin | Larkspur Oven
Description:     Fresh bread for Austin restaurants and cafes, baked overnight and delivered
                 before 7 am. Order by 9 pm. Ask for a sample box.
H2s:             Bread delivered before 7 am | A menu built with your chef | Simple standing
                 orders | Frequently asked questions
```
